Berkeley filled this part of the Bay in the 1960s to build a regional park, using state and federal recreation money. The city now wants to charge for parking in the lots that serve the South Basin launches ($6 a day or $500 a year for 273 spaces). It has also proposed a pier ferry terminal that would convert 275 spaces in existing waterfront lots to paid, all-day parking for ferry riders. The city cites a shortfall in the marina fund to justify both. *(Report pp. 6–7)*

**Why the report calls it the most serious threat.** The report says this would be BCDC's first standalone permit for parking fees. It warns that approval would give every agency holding shoreline land a template for charging people to reach the water. *(pp. 1, 9)*

**Parking demand.** About 171,000 people a year visit the south waterfront, and many drive because they carry gear or bring kids. City consultants forecast about 915 ferry riders a day, with 48–60% driving, and riders would compete with recreational visitors for the same lots. The report argues that the city's parking counts were taken at low-use times. *(pp. 7–8)*

**Equity.** The south lots serve more lower-income visitors than the north lots (20% of south-lot respondents earn under $75,000, against 8% in the north). BCDC guidance says public access to the Bay should "in general" be free of charge. *(pp. 7–8)*

**Policy basis cited by the report.** Bay Plan Recreation Policy 3 calls for "sufficient, convenient parking" at non-motorized launch sites. Recreation Policy 9 says park and marina parking "should not be usurped by ferry patrons." The marina also sits on public-trust tidelands. *(pp. 7, 30)*
